McDonald’s, country by country

63 countries and territories · 429 menu items · 38 sold in more than one country · sourced 2026-09-21

Every McDonald’s market sells the same core menu — Big Mac, Quarter Pounder, McNuggets, fries — and then fills the rest of the board with things invented for the country it is in. This page is the second half of that: the 429 items that a country’s own menu is documented as having, gathered from Wikipedia’s country-by-country article on McDonald’s availability, and the 38 that turn up in more than one country.

The count is per country, so it rewards being written about. Malaysia has 43 documented items and Venezuela has 3; that is a statement about the source article, not about the menus. The overlap column is the more reliable one: an item counted in five countries is in five countries.

Where they overlap

38 items are documented in more than one country, 391 in exactly one. The widest single item is the McVeggie burger, in 9 countries — Hong Kong, India, Malaysia, Thailand, Greece, Italy, Portugal, Slovenia, Australia. Behind it sit the items that migrate with the brand rather than with a market: regional staples sold under one name across neighbouring countries, and single products that were rolled out across several markets at once.

This is the closest thing here to “the local menu”. It is also the column to distrust most, because an item is only listed as one-country if the sources never mention it anywhere else — a hole in the source reads the same as a hole in the menu.

How the data was sourced

  • The base record is Wikipedia’s International availability of McDonald’s products (revision 1375996888, 2026-09-21), which has one prose section per country — 63 sections across six continents. Each section’s text was pulled from the article’s own HTML, with image captions, references and edit links stripped, and the menu items named in that text extracted country by country.
  • Each extracted item was then checked mechanically against the section text it came from: every item attributed to a country’s own section is a literal substring of that section. Items that failed that check were dropped rather than shown. Items taken from the second article’s availability statements carry that provenance in the table instead.
  • The core menu is excluded by a fixed list (Big Mac, Quarter Pounder, McChicken, McNuggets, Filet-O-Fish, fries, McDouble, McFlurry, McMuffin, Happy Meal, Big Tasty and the drinks) — 4 mentions were dropped for naming one of those rather than something local. A locally named variant of one of them (Chicken Maharaja Mac, Chicken Big Mac, Fish Mac) is kept.
  • The overlap column leans on a second article, List of McDonald’s products (revision 1373545114, 2026-09-06), which is written product-first and repeatedly says which countries sell a given item (“Available in India, Slovenia, Greece, Malaysia, Portugal, and Australia”). Those sentences were parsed for country names, giving 34 extra country-item pairings that the country sections never make explicit. Both articles are parsed by scripts kept with this dataset.
  • Country names from the second article were folded into the first article’s sections, so “the UK” and “Ireland” both count as the source’s combined United Kingdom and Ireland section, and Hawaii, Guam and Puerto Rico count as the United States. Turkey is the source’s Turkey, whose section also covers Türkiye branding.
  • Where the two articles spell the same product differently, the spellings are merged rather than double-counted: “mc aloo tikki” with “McAloo Tikki”, “mc veggie” with “McVeggie burger”, “mcveggie” with “McVeggie burger”, “mcveggie burger” with “McVeggie burger”. Names that are generic on both sides — “onion rings”, “chicken wings”, “arepas” — are matched as written, so those overlaps mean the same dish, not necessarily the same product.
  • Caveats. The source article is uneven: Malaysia’s section is 43 items long, Serbia’s is 1, and its house style is to describe what is interesting locally rather than to enumerate a menu — so these are lower bounds, not menu sizes. Items are a mix of current, limited-time and discontinued products, and none of it is dated per item. 63 of the world’s roughly 100 McDonald’s markets have a section here; the rest are absent, not empty.
