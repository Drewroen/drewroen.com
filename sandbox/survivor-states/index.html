Survivor, state by state

50 completed seasons · 917 appearances · 50 titles · 2000 – 2026

Every contestant on Survivor is introduced with a hometown, and that hometown is the only thing tying the show’s 917 appearances to a map. Read that way, 47 states and the District of Columbia have sent someone to the island across 50 completed seasons, 760 people in all, and they have produced the show’s 50 Sole Survivor titles.

The obvious answer is California: 8 of the 50 titles, more than any other state by a distance, and 224 appearances. But California is also the largest state in the country, so the interesting question is who beats its weight — and on that, New Jersey wins: 4 titles from 28 appearances, the best strike rate of any state with a real sample, and roughly 2.6 titles more than its share of the population predicts.

How the data was sourced

  • The base record is Wikipedia’s List of Survivor (American TV series) contestants, which lists every contestant by name, age, hometown, profession, season and finish. Its wikitext was parsed directly: 938 appearance rows across six decade blocks, with rowspan cells expanded so each season’s column resolves correctly.
  • Hometowns are written as “City, ST” in a template, so the state code is read from the template rather than sliced out of the rendered text. Finishing positions come from the sort key the table already carries, which is what keeps the six seasons with joint runners-up (2.5) and the worded finishes (“Winner”, “Runner-up”) in their real positions instead of dropping to zero.
  • Checks run before publishing: all 50 season winners were diffed against the winner field in each season’s own article — 50 of 50 match. Within every season the largest finishing position equals the number of contestants on the list — true in all 50 seasons — so the cast sizes that the “avg finish” column depends on are internally consistent. And the titles sum to 50, exactly one per completed season.
  • Caveats. The state is the residence the contestant gave for that season, not where they were born or live now, so the same person can be counted for two states: Sandra Diaz-Twine appears for Washington, North Carolina and Florida, Aubry Bracco for Massachusetts, California and New Hampshire. Three states have never had a contestant in any season — Alaska, North Dakota and New Mexico — and West Virginia’s first is in the season airing as this was written, so it is absent here until that season finishes.
  • Season 51 is excluded entirely: it has played out one vote, which is not a season. Its single elimination and its twenty unplaced contestants are left out of every number above.
  • The honest summary: 917 appearances spread over 47 states means most states have a sample of one to five people. Differences of a few percentage points in win rate, or a few tenths on the adjusted column, are noise. California’s 8 titles and New Jersey’s rate are the two findings big enough to survive that caveat, and both point the same way: take the two coasts.
