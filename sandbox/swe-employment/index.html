Software developer employment in the United States

BLS OEWS national estimates · May 2006 – May 2025 · counts jobs, not people or postings

Each point is the number of jobs held by software developers in the United States in May of that year: 801,580 in 2006, 1,687,890 in 2025 — an increase of 886,310 (110.6%). The numbers come from the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS) program, which surveys employers rather than households.

What it counts: jobs, not people. The survey covers part-time and full-time workers paid a wage or salary, so one person holding two covered jobs counts twice, and the self-employed, owners and partners in unincorporated firms, household workers and unpaid family workers are not counted at all. Each value is a May snapshot, not an annual average, and it is not job postings or industry payroll totals.

One wrinkle: in 2019 and 2020 BLS published software developers together with software QA analysts and testers in a single code, then split QA out again in 2021. That makes the 2020 → 2021 step look like a decline (1,476,800 to 1,364,180) when most of it is definitional — add QA back and 2021 was 1,554,300, up 5.2% on 2020, and 2022 was up another 11.4%.

Hiring and layoffs in tech

The same story from the industry side: JOLTS hires and layoffs & discharges for the Information sector (NAICS 51) — the conventional “tech” cut, about 2.9 million workers. This is an industry measure, not an occupation one, so it cannot say how many software developers were hired or laid off, and most developers actually sit in computer systems design (NAICS 5415), which JOLTS does not publish separately.

Source levels are in thousands (JTS510000000000000HIL, JTS510000000000000LDL); annual totals are the sum of the twelve monthly values × 1,000. “Layoffs and discharges” means involuntary separations initiated by the employer — layoffs with no intent to rehire, absences over seven days, closures, mergers and firings — not just announced layoff rounds.

How the data was sourced

  • One point per May survey, read from that year’s OEWS national estimates workbook (national_M{YYYY}_dl.xls / .xlsx) in BLS’s oesm{YY}nat.zip, using the TOT_EMP column for whichever software-developer occupation code(s) BLS published that year.
  • Where the occupation was split across two codes (2006–2018), employment is the sum of the two rows. 2019–2020 used the combined code 15-1256; 2021 on uses 15-1252.
  • The current release file was taken from BLS directly. The older zip files were retrieved from the Internet Archive’s raw snapshots of the same BLS URLs, because bls.gov returns 403 to this machine’s IP range.
  • Checks run before publishing: all 20 years re-read from the raw workbooks match the charted series exactly; May 2018 also matches BLS’s published static national table (903,160 applications + 405,330 systems software = 1,308,490); the latest release on BLS’s list is May 2025, which is the 1,687,890 shown here.
  • QA analysts and testers (15-1253) were read from the same files: 190,120 (2021), 196,420 (2022), 203,040 (2023), 199,800 (2024), 186,740 (2025). Adding them back is the closest available continuation of the 2019–2020 combined code.
  • The JOLTS section applies the same discipline on the industry side: hires (JTS510000000000000HIL) and layoffs and discharges (JTS510000000000000LDL) for Information, levels in thousands, not seasonally adjusted. Each annual total is the sum of that year’s twelve monthly values × 1,000, and any year without twelve months is dropped — which is why 2026 is absent.
  • Both JOLTS series were checked by implied employment (level ÷ rate ≈ 2.9 million, the Information sector’s size) before being used. A hand-built JOLTS series ID resolves to some population and returns plausible-looking numbers for the wrong one; one such mistake earlier in this work pulled a series roughly twice the size of Information, so the check is not optional.
  • JOLTS numbers move: a first-published month is preliminary, revised the following month, and revised again in the annual update, so the most recent years carry the most uncertainty.
  • Caveats: OEWS estimates are not revised after release, and no single basis is perfectly consistent for the whole window — the 2019–2020 code includes QA while the developer codes before and after it do not. So the 2020→2021 fall from 1,476,800 to 1,364,180 is definitional rather than economic: QA analysts and testers moved to their own code (15-1253) in 2021 and account for most of the gap.
